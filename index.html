Fetal Distress

Signs of fetal hypoxia/acidosis requiring urgent intervention. Master FHR patterns (late/variable decelerations, tachycardia, bradycardia), meconium, intrauterine resuscitation, and nursing management.

Definition & Causes of Fetal Distress

Fetal distress (now often termed non‑reassuring fetal status) is a condition in which the fetus shows evidence of hypoxia, acidosis, or compromise that may lead to permanent injury or death if not promptly addressed.
  • Maternal causes: Hypertension, hypotension, diabetes, anemia, hypoxemia, seizures, trauma, fever, tachyarrhythmias.
  • Placental causes: Abruption, previa, insufficiency, infarction, post‑term pregnancy.
  • Umbilical cord causes: Prolapse, true knot, nuchal cord, compression, short cord.
  • Fetal causes: Anemia, infection (chorioamnionitis), arrhythmia, congenital anomalies.
  • Labor‑related causes: Uterine hyperstimulation (tachysystole), prolonged labor, rapid descent, medications (epidural hypotension).

FHR Patterns & Decelerations

  • Baseline FHR: 110–160 bpm. Tachycardia (>160 bpm) – early sign of hypoxia, infection, drugs. Bradycardia (<110 bpm) – severe hypoxia, cord compression, maternal hypotension.
  • Variability: Moderate (6–25 bpm) is reassuring. Minimal or absent variability suggests acidemia or CNS depression.
  • Early decelerations: Mirror contraction, due to head compression – benign.
  • Late decelerations: Begin after contraction peak, return after contraction ends – uteroplacental insufficiency – ominous.
  • Variable decelerations: Abrupt, variable shape – cord compression.
  • Prolonged deceleration: Lasts 2–10 minutes – urgent evaluation.
  • Significance: Late decelerations + absent variability = severe fetal distress, impending acidosis.
⚠️ Interventions for late/variable decelerations: Lateral position, oxygen, IV fluids, stop oxytocin, consider amnioinfusion.

Meconium & Other Signs of Fetal Compromise

  • Meconium‑stained amniotic fluid: Passage of meconium before delivery suggests fetal distress, especially if thick (pea soup). Associated with post‑term pregnancy, IUGR, cord compression, placental insufficiency.
  • Fetal acidosis: Scalp pH <7.20 confirms hypoxia. Fetal scalp blood sampling (rarely done now).
  • Decreased fetal movement: Reduced activity can indicate chronic fetal compromise.
  • Abnormal biophysical profile (BPP): Score ≤4 indicates acidosis.
  • Elevated fetal lactate or base deficit.
📌 Meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS): Thick meconium can cause airway obstruction, pneumonitis. Bulb suction, deep tracheal suction if thick meconium and non‑vigorous newborn.

Intrauterine Resuscitation

  • Maternal repositioning: Left lateral – relieves vena cava compression, improves placental blood flow.
  • Oxygen administration: 8–10 L/min via non‑rebreather mask – increases fetal oxygen delivery.
  • IV fluid bolus: Treats maternal hypotension (especially after epidural).
  • Reduce uterine activity: Stop or decrease oxytocin, administer tocolytic (terbutaline 0.25 mg subQ) for tachysystole.
  • Amnioinfusion: For recurrent variable decelerations (cord compression) – infuse warm saline into uterus via intrauterine catheter.
  • Fetal scalp stimulation: FHR acceleration after scalp stimulation indicates well‑being.
  • Delivery: If persistent non‑reassuring pattern despite resuscitation, expedite delivery via operative vaginal or cesarean section.

Nursing Role & Documentation

  • Recognition: Interpret FHR tracing (baseline, variability, decelerations).
  • Immediate actions: Change maternal position (left lateral), increase IV fluids, apply oxygen, discontinue oxytocin, notify provider.
  • Monitor maternal vital signs, contraction frequency, and fetal response.
  • Document: Time of onset, interventions, FHR pattern, fetal blood sampling results (if done), communication with provider, and delivery decisions.
  • Prepare for expedited delivery: Assist with vacuum/forceps or cesarean, have NICU team ready for neonatal resuscitation.
  • Emotional support: Explain interventions, reduce maternal anxiety, involve partner.
